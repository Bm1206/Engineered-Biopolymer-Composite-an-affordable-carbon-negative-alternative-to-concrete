# Engineered Biopolymer Composite, an affordable carbon negative alternative to concrete

<p><b><i>Authors: Barney H. Miao (Stanford University), Andrew C. Lesh (Stanford University), Paul Hart (nano@stanford), Steven Song (Stanford University), David J. Loftus (NASA Ames Research Center), Michael D. Lepech (Stanford University)</i></b></p>

This repository contains data related to the publication entitled "Engineered Biopolymer Composite, an affordable carbon-negative alternative to concrete." Included here are all data (i.e., experimental results, microstructural data, etc.) that were used for our study. Additional data is included in our Supplementary Material file entitled, "Supplementary Material.pdf" , which goes into greater detail regarding the design, manufacture, experimental procedure, and techno-economic life cycle assessment conducted in the study.

**Engineered Biopolymer Composite (EBC)** is a novel construction material that valorizes waste-stream lignin generated by major industrial processes. EBC is manufactured by mixing constituent materials (aggregate, biopolymer and solvent), followed by molding. The molding process includes a  pre-compaction step to consolidate the wet material. As the material is dried out, it gains strength. EBC products can be used in a range of structural applications, including pavement, structural elements and block systems, as alternatives to conventional concrete and bricks, thereby reducing reliance on cementitious materials and clay products which are associated with environmental and human health burdens. An important aspect of EBC is the sequestration of carbon-rich biopolymers, contributing to a life-cycle carbon-negative footprint. Ultimately, the development of EBC represents a paradigm shift in decarbonization strategies by introducing a pathway that utilizes bio-based waste streams to enable a more sustainable built environment.
